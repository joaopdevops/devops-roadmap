## 🧮 Binary and subnet masks — why the math works

- An octet is 8 bits worth **128-64-32-16-8-4-2-1**. 2⁸ = 256 combinations, so an octet goes from 0 to 255.
- A mask is just bits turned on from left to right: `/26` = `11111111.11111111.11111111.11000000` = `255.255.255.192`.
- Borrowed bits → 2ⁿ subnets. Host bits → 2ⁿ addresses (minus network and broadcast).
- **Finding the subnet of an IP:** list the multiples of the block size; the network is the largest one that doesn't pass the IP; broadcast = next network − 1. Sanity check: block × subnets = 256.
- Real-world use: an ISP gives a `/30` to a customer point-to-point link and a `/29` when the customer needs 5 public IPs.
